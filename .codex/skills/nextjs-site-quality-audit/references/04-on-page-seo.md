# 04 — On-page SEO and Content Audit

On-page SEO must be judged against the site's business model, brand identity, audience, and route intent. Do not keyword-stuff or recommend generic copy additions.

## 1. Page intent alignment

For each important page, identify:

- Primary purpose.
- Likely search intent: informational, commercial, transactional, navigational, local, branded.
- Target audience.
- Conversion action.
- Required trust signals.

Check whether:

- The H1, intro, metadata, and first meaningful section all agree.
- The page answers the user's likely pre-conversion questions.
- The page gives enough context before asking for conversion.
- The page has a clear next action.

## 2. Headings and structure

Check:

- One clear H1 per page or route template.
- H1 is specific, visible, and aligned with metadata.
- H2/H3 structure is logical and descriptive.
- Headings are not used only for styling.
- Sections follow a useful narrative order.
- Reusable components do not inject duplicate H1s.
- Dynamic content templates do not create empty or repeated headings.

## 3. Content quality and coverage

Check:

- Core offer/product/service is explained concretely.
- Copy avoids vague claims without proof.
- Benefits are connected to features/process/results.
- Thin pages have a reason to exist or should be consolidated.
- Important services/products have dedicated sections or pages where search demand justifies it.
- FAQs answer real objections or operational questions.
- Content is not duplicated across many pages with only small word swaps.
- Content is understandable to the target customer, not just developers or insiders.

## 4. Brand and semantic consistency

Check:

- Brand name is consistent across title, metadata, logo alt, schema, footer, and legal pages.
- Tone of voice matches the business and audience.
- Visual claims are supported by copy.
- Metadata does not use terms absent from body content.
- Service/product names are consistent across navigation, cards, page sections, and schema.

## 5. Conversion and UX content

Check:

- Primary CTA is visible and specific.
- CTA text is meaningful, not only "Click here" or "Submit".
- Contact/reservation/quote forms explain what happens after submission.
- Phone/email/address are easy to find where relevant.
- Trust signals appear before or near high-friction CTAs.
- Pricing, process, timing, availability, or booking requirements are clear when relevant.
- Error and success copy is useful.

## 6. Internal links and content hubs

Check:

- Important service/product/project pages are linked contextually.
- Related content is linked where it helps users.
- Breadcrumbs are visible when hierarchy matters.
- Blog/article pages link back to relevant services or conversion pages.
- Portfolio/case study pages link to relevant technologies/services.
- Anchor text describes destination and value.

## 7. E-E-A-T and trust signals

Check where relevant:

- About/company information.
- Author information for articles.
- Project/case study evidence.
- Testimonials/reviews with appropriate attribution.
- Certifications, press, partners, or years of experience.
- Legal/privacy/cookie/contact information.
- Clear ownership and support channels.
- For sensitive topics, stronger author/reviewer credentials.

## 8. Image/content alignment

Check:

- Images reinforce the page topic and brand.
- Hero images are not purely decorative if they carry sales meaning.
- Galleries have contextual captions or nearby text where useful.
- Product/project images are paired with useful descriptions.
- Alt text supports accessibility first and SEO second.

## 9. Local intent content

For local businesses:

- The page clearly states location and service area.
- Opening hours, address, phone, booking/contact flows are visible.
- Local landmarks, delivery areas, or service regions are included only if useful and truthful.
- Local schema matches visible NAP information.
- Location pages contain unique details, not doorway content.

## 10. Article/blog content

Check:

- Title and H1 are specific and promise a clear outcome.
- Intro establishes problem and audience quickly.
- Sections answer real questions.
- Content includes practical examples where useful.
- Author/date/update information is appropriate.
- Article schema is present only when content qualifies.
- Related content and conversion paths are included.
